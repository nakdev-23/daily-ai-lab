# Image to Video — make images move

> **Image-to-Video** (turning a still image into video) is one of Runway's most popular features — just upload an image and the AI creates a realistic video.

---

## What is Image-to-Video?

**Image-to-Video** is the process where the AI analyzes your still image, then creates natural motion for it.

For example:
- A still sea image → a video of rippling sea waves
- A photo of a person standing → a video of the person walking or moving
- A forest image → a video of leaves rustling in the wind
- A food image → a video of steam rising from the hot food

---

## Models used in Image-to-Video

### Gen-4.5 (recommended)
- Highest quality, very realistic motion
- Uses 12 credits/second
- Supports Landscape (1280x720) and Portrait (720x1280)

### Gen-4 Turbo
- Faster, more economical
- Uses 5 credits/second
- Good for experimenting or draft work

---

## How to use Image-to-Video

### Step 1: Prepare the image

A good image for Image-to-Video should:
- **Be at least 640x640 pixels**
- Not exceed **4K resolution** (4,096 x 4,096 pixels)
- File formats: **JPEG, PNG, or WebP**
- File size no more than **16MB**

### Step 2: Upload the image

1. On the Runway page, click **"Image to Video"**
2. Click **"Upload Image"** or drag a file in
3. Wait for the image to finish uploading

### Step 3: Add a Prompt (optional but recommended)

Although you don't have to add a Prompt, adding a description helps the video be more on-target.

**Example Prompts for Image-to-Video:**

If the image is a beach:
```
Gentle ocean waves rolling in, warm breeze, 
seagulls flying in the background, golden sunset
```

If the image is a character:
```
Character smiling and waving at camera, 
natural movement, soft lighting
```

### Step 4: Set the Duration and Aspect Ratio

- **Duration**: 5 or 10 seconds
- **Aspect Ratio**: choose to match the source image

> **Important:** If the Aspect Ratio doesn't match, Runway will **Auto-crop** (automatically crop the edges from the center), which may cut off important parts of the image.

### Step 5: Generate

Click **"Generate"** and wait for the result.

## Techniques for choosing images for good results

### Suitable images
- Images with a clear subject distinguishable from the background
- Images with elements that move naturally (water, leaves, hair)
- Images with good lighting and shadows
- Images with no text or watermark

### Images to avoid
- Low-quality or very blurry images
- Images with overly complex composition
- Images shot in low light (Dark/Low light)
- Cartoon or Illustration images with complex line work

## Motion Brush with Image-to-Video

**Motion Brush** (a motion-control brush) can be used together with Image-to-Video to control which part of the image should move (read more in the Motion Brush chapter).

---

## First Frame / Last Frame — control the start and end points

**First Frame** (the first image of the video) and **Last Frame** (the last image of the video) let you define where the video starts and ends.

How to use it:
1. Upload the First Frame (the starting image)
2. Upload the Last Frame (the ending image)
3. The AI creates motion connecting the two images

Benefits:
- Create a Transition between two scenes
- Control which pose a character starts and ends in
- Create Morphing from one object to another

---

## Limitations

- The AI tries to create natural motion, but sometimes there may be distortion in complex parts
- Real people's faces may sometimes look strange after creating motion
- Images with lots of detail may make the result hard to predict

---

## Summary

Image-to-Video is a very powerful feature for creating video content without filming from scratch. The key is choosing a good-quality image with naturally movable elements, combined with writing a clear Prompt to control the direction of the motion.
