# Camera Controls — control camera motion

> **Camera Controls** in Runway let you define how the camera moves in an AI-generated video — like being a cinematographer yourself.

---

## Why control the camera?

Good camera motion makes a video:
- Look more professional and interesting
- Tell a story better
- Convey the desired emotion

---

## Types of camera motion in Runway

### 1. Pan (rotating the camera left-right horizontally)
The camera rotates left or right while the camera body stays still.

**Use when:** shooting a panorama, or tracking a horizontally moving subject
```
Pan left — the camera rotates left
Pan right — the camera rotates right
```

### 2. Tilt (rotating the camera up-down)
The camera rotates up or down.

**Use when:** shooting tall buildings, revealing the environment, or conveying grandeur
```
Tilt up — the camera rotates up
Tilt down — the camera rotates down
```

### 3. Zoom (enlarging/reducing the image)
Enlarge toward the subject or pull back.

**Use when:** emphasizing detail or revealing a wide scene
```
Zoom in — the image enlarges
Zoom out — the image shrinks, showing more context
```

### 4. Dolly (physically moving the camera in/out)
The camera physically moves toward or away from the subject (unlike Zoom, which is just lens magnification).

```
Dolly in — the camera moves toward the subject
Dolly out — the camera moves away from the subject
```

### 5. Truck (moving the camera parallel horizontally)
The camera moves parallel to the subject, to the left or right.

```
Truck left — the camera moves left
Truck right — the camera moves right
```

### 6. Pedestal (moving the camera straight up-down)
The camera moves straight up or down vertically.

```
Pedestal up — the camera goes up
Pedestal down — the camera goes down
```

### 7. Roll (rotating the camera around the front-back axis)
The camera rotates, tilting left or right, making the image tilt.

**Use when:** conveying imbalance, or creating special effects

---

## How to use Camera Controls in Runway

### Via the Prompt
The easiest way is to write it directly in the Prompt:

```
Slow dolly in toward the subject, cinematic movement
```

```
Camera slowly pans right to reveal the cityscape
```

### Via the Camera Controls Panel
1. After uploading an image or setting a Prompt
2. Find the **"Camera Motion"** or **"Camera Controls"** section
3. Choose the type of motion you want
4. Adjust the **Intensity** (the speed and distance of the motion)

---

## Combining camera motions

You can combine several types at once:

```
Slow dolly in + slight pan right + tilt up
```

Combinations popular in film:
- **Dolly in + Zoom out** = Dolly Zoom / Vertigo Effect (a dizzying effect — the background enlarges while the foreground subject grows)
- **Pan + Tilt** = Arc shot
- **Truck + Tilt up** = Walking reveal (revealing something grand)

## Example Camera Movements for each mood

| Desired mood | Recommended camera motion |
|---|---|
| Grand / epic | Slow tilt up, Wide aerial shot |
| Mysterious / scary | Slow dolly in, Low angle |
| Romantic | Slow pan, Soft focus |
| Exciting / Action | Fast zoom, Shaky cam |
| Peaceful / relaxed | Static shot, Slow pan |
| Revealing the hidden | Slow dolly out, Reveal shot |

## Camera Movements used in Prompts

**Commonly used words in Prompts:**

```
establishing shot (an opening shot showing the environment)
tracking shot (the camera follows a subject)
overhead shot / bird's eye view (looking from above)
worm's eye view (looking from below)
POV shot (Point of View — a first-person view)
over-the-shoulder shot
crane shot (the camera moves up and down vertically)
steadicam (the camera moves smoothly without shaking)
handheld (a handheld camera — slightly shaky, looks realistic)
```

---

## Tips from directors

### Always use "Slow" for professional work
```
Slow, smooth dolly in
```
Expensive-looking videos usually use slow, smooth camera motion.

### Avoid too many simultaneous motions
If you put in too much motion at once, the AI may produce confusing results.

### Specify the Speed in the description
- "very slow"
- "gradually"
- "rapidly"
- "swift"
